Abstract

This dissertation investigates the construction of a Kazakh text corpus and the development of a system for identifying five functional styles: official and business, scientific, publicistic, literary and colloquial. The engineering problem includes heterogeneous source formats, incomplete extraction, source-derived labels, duplicate publications and substantial differences in document length. The research problem is to distinguish stylistic generalisation from recognition of source-specific vocabulary, document templates and genre distributions.

The current document bank, {{CORPUS_ID}}, contains {{CORPUS_N}} provisionally accepted documents with provenance and versioned processing records. It is distinct from the smaller fixed datasets used in the completed experiments. Six classifier variants have been compared on the same development inputs: three TF-IDF pipelines, multilingual BERT and two pooling variants of a Kazakh RoBERTa encoder. Repeated runs and a separate source-holdout diagnostic are available. These results establish a working system and a reproducible experimental foundation, while their preliminary reference labels limit claims about accuracy on arbitrary Kazakh text. The remaining evaluation must preserve that distinction rather than reinterpret internal development scores as expert-validated performance.

1 Introduction

1.1 Problem and scope

Text style identification assigns a communicative register to a document. A report about a scientific discovery can be publicistic, a personal question about government policy can be colloquial, and a news report quoting a law is not automatically an official document. Consequently, topic, publisher and functional style should not be treated as interchangeable labels. This distinction is central to collecting data from the web, where source sections provide useful candidate labels but do not reliably establish the style of every page.

KazStyle addresses two connected tasks. The first is corpus construction: recovering usable text, recording its origin, organising style and genre information, and controlling repeated content. The second is classification: learning a mapping from the text to one of five style labels and exposing the result through a local application. The website is a demonstration and inspection interface. Models are trained on text records; they are not trained on websites as software applications, URLs or screenshots.

1.2 Aim and research questions

The aim is to construct a traceable Kazakh corpus and develop a reproducible text style identification system. The study asks: how strongly do source, genre and length affect measured performance; whether Kazakh-specific and modern multilingual encoders improve on transparent linear baselines under a shared input protocol; and how classifiers behave on reserved documents and different sources. These questions keep the research aligned with the approved thesis topic without requiring a new general-purpose language model to be pretrained from scratch.

The intended contributions are a documented corpus-building procedure, explicit records of extraction and labelling limitations, a controlled comparison of classifiers, and an operational local system. A claim to the first Kazakh corpus annotated by functional style is not established. Existing national-corpus infrastructure already uses stylistic metadata [1]. The contribution must therefore be stated in terms of the actual data release, reproducibility and evaluation conditions.

2 Background and methodological choices

2.1 Functional styles and annotation boundaries

Official and business texts formalise rights, obligations, decisions or administrative requests. Scientific texts explain concepts, methods, evidence or research results. Publicistic texts report or discuss socially relevant events for a public audience. Literary texts construct narratives, characters or poetic images. Colloquial texts serve direct informal interaction. These operational definitions accommodate a school application as an official text and a short explanation of photosynthesis as scientific or educational-scientific text when its explanatory function is clear.

Not every item can be assigned unambiguously. The review scheme therefore includes mixed style, insufficient context, predominantly non-Kazakh content and damaged or non-content text. Reviewers are asked to use the communicative function of the shown passage rather than one keyword or knowledge of the publisher. A source-derived sampling stratum remains separate from a later review decision.

2.2 Linear and transformer classifiers

TF-IDF represents a text numerically. Logistic Regression or Linear SVM then predicts a label from that representation. A pipeline called TF-IDF plus SVM is one complete classifier, not an incomplete combination of unrelated models. Word features provide a transparent lexical baseline. Character features offer a complementary representation of recurring word fragments and orthographic patterns. Their usefulness for this task must be established experimentally; it is not inferred solely from the morphological richness of Kazakh.

Transformer encoders use their own tokenizers and contextual representations. The implemented Kazakh encoder is kz-transformers/kaz-roberta-conversational [2]; it must be named Kaz-RoBERTa rather than KazBERT. Both the encoder and a five-label classification head are fine-tuned. The completed experiments include a last-layer representation and concatenation of the last four layers. The latter is an ablation to be measured, not an architectural improvement assumed in advance.

A current candidate for extending the comparison is jhu-clsp/mmBERT-small, a multilingual encoder with approximately 140 million parameters [3, 4]. The candidate has not yet been trained or evaluated in this project. Compatibility, tokenisation and memory must be checked before it enters the frozen experiment plan. Ordinary ModernBERT-base is trained primarily on English and code [5]; the fact that it is newer does not establish its suitability for Kazakh. A larger model or an LLM is useful only if it answers a defined research question within the available computational budget.

3 Corpus construction

3.1 Data layers and current composition

The corpus is organised into raw source snapshots, extracted candidates, screened full documents and model-specific input views. These layers are versioned separately. A candidate is not counted as a usable document merely because it was downloaded. A fragment of a book is not counted as a new independent work. Re-extracting an existing page repairs that document rather than increasing the number of collected documents.

Numerical balance is one design consideration, but equal class counts cannot compensate for narrow genre coverage. In the preceding bank, official texts were dominated by legal acts, scientific texts by research abstracts, and literary texts by poetry. Colloquial messages were substantially shorter than the other classes. Expansion therefore prioritises underrepresented genres and sources rather than collecting more news or duplicating existing material to meet a quota.

3.2 Text preservation and extraction quality

Processing removes HTML navigation, scripts, contact details, addresses and identified boilerplate from the model text. URLs and other provenance fields remain in metadata for tracing and grouping. Kazakh letters, meaningful endings, punctuation, names and dates should not be deleted simply because they may correlate with a style. Cleaning is inspected for preservation of content, not judged solely by whether a file can be opened as a spreadsheet.

The audit on 18 October identified an extraction failure with direct consequences for text integrity. On a folklore site, ordinary words were rendered as interactive vocabulary buttons. Removing every HTML button deleted those words from the story. The revised parser unwraps the recognised vocabulary widgets while still removing player controls and forms. Saved HTML allowed 1,827 words to be restored in 126 existing stories. Each proposed repair is constrained by the original extracted-text hash, the HTML hash, the expected number of vocabulary widgets and the repaired-text hash. No language model is used to invent missing words.

A second inspection concerned apparent gaps in a prose excerpt. Comparison with the cached source showed that the words were present and that the visible issue was whitespace around inline punctuation. This case was not treated as evidence of missing content. Keeping failed hypotheses in the audit prevents speculative repairs from silently changing the corpus.

3.3 Labels, provenance and duplicates

Most initial labels originate from source categories. Assistant screening is recorded as AI-assisted curation. It is not described as two-person expert annotation, and the two types of evidence are never merged. In a source/genre/length-stratified pilot, the assistant read 25 shown texts, five from each source-derived style stratum. Seven were excerpts. One birthday notice in a research journal was incompatible with its scientific candidate label. This finding motivates further review of journal content, but a small purposive sample does not estimate the error rate of the entire corpus.

A subsequent targeted curation pass examined 888 previously pending colloquial candidates in full. Selection covered forum questions, answers and comments, and four upstream customer-review strata; only texts of 8–55 whitespace-delimited words were eligible. The assistant retained 810 candidates semantically and excluded or deferred 78, including copied exercises, predominantly quoted lyrics, promotional passages and ambiguous written registers. This is an inclusion decision under an explicit scope, not an estimate of annotation accuracy. Topic, sentiment and star rating were not used as style labels. Spelling variation, natural code switching and meaningful emojis were retained.

The revised export added those 810 documents and returned 27 previously accepted documents to review because Unicode or mixed-script source addresses had escaped the earlier detector. It did not rewrite the retained earlier texts. The unified_corpus_20261018b revision contains 2,053 colloquial documents. These repairs and additions establish a larger curation base; they do not establish improved classifier accuracy before a new frozen experiment is completed. The operational guidelines were consolidated after these initial rounds and must be frozen before subsequent reference annotation, rather than described as retrospectively preregistered.

Exact and near-duplicate content, parent documents, known authors, works and document-template families are grouped before splitting. A legal passage quoted in a news article can connect documents from different source categories; such links require review rather than independent random allocation. Unknown authorship and undiscovered paraphrases remain limitations. The exporter also distinguishes an absent group identifier, represented by JSON null, from a literal identifier. Non-standard NaN values must not be written as if they were portable JSON metadata.

The principal CSV has text and label columns. Metadata supplies document identifiers, origin, genre, review status, hashes and group information. CSV quoting, unique identifiers, per-style exports and alignment with metadata are checked programmatically. These checks establish structural integrity; they do not prove that every style label is correct. Publicly accessible text is not automatically licensed for redistribution. Release decisions therefore distinguish metadata and processing recipes from copyrighted full texts.

Collection and preparation reside in the data package, learning procedures in the training package, saved-model loading in inference, and the HTTP service in the API package. The frontend uses that service rather than a separate classifier. The deployment profile names the dataset and model runs and records weight hashes. Active models and archived research runs are resolved explicitly, so moving an inactive experiment does not require rewriting its scientific results.

The stable deployment remains the v4 profile, based on 610 fixed text fragments: 370 training, 120 validation and 120 development-test examples. A later v6 comparison uses 1,265 fixed fragments: 980 training, 140 validation and 145 development-test examples. These are modelling datasets, not alternative counts of the full document bank. Each completed family was investigated with seeds 42, 43 and 44. The representative deployment seed was fixed in advance as 42.

All classifiers in a comparison receive the same text view and split. TF-IDF is fitted only on training data. Epoch selection uses validation Macro-F1, and model-family selection uses mean validation performance across all planned seeds. Previously inspected test sets are development diagnostics. Familiar examples, including the school application and photosynthesis passage, are regression cases; they do not become new unseen tests simply by being submitted through the website again.

Macro-F1 is the unweighted mean of per-class F1. It is reported with per-class precision, recall and F1, confusion matrices, seed variability and group-based uncertainty estimates where supported. Repeated seeds share a dataset and do not constitute independent samples from the target population. Source-holdout and length diagnostics address additional failure modes but require their own dataset descriptions. Their numerical differences from the main experiment cannot be interpreted as a controlled effect when training volume and test composition also change.

The primary comparison operates on fixed excerpts with explicit token limits. The full original document is retained separately. Long-document window aggregation is a secondary research question and must be registered before selecting an aggregation rule using evaluation outcomes. A model must not silently discard input and then present its result as an assessment of the complete document.

A high internal score is compatible with a small or source-correlated test. It is not, by itself, proof of leakage, nor proof of broad stylistic competence. The observed errors, label provenance and source distributions must be considered together. In particular, the v6 Kaz-RoBERTa model assigns the familiar school application to the colloquial class, while the stable v4 model gives the official class. This regression is why the higher-scoring development version did not automatically replace the site model.

5.2 Separate source-holdout diagnostic

The next experiment trained separate classifiers using a different split: 400 training, 100 validation and 150 test examples, with separated domains and groups under its recorded protocol. It provides evidence about the selected source-shift scenario. It is not a final unknown expert-labelled benchmark and does not isolate source shift from all other dataset differences.

{{HOLDOUT_TABLE}}

The diagnostic supports retaining strong character and word baselines and examining genre-specific errors. It does not justify replacing a classifier solely because one manually selected example is correct. Similarly, the morphology ablation using Stanza did not establish a stable improvement over the corresponding raw-text baselines on its fixed v4 data. Reporting this negative result is more informative than repeating preprocessing experiments until a preferred number appears.

5.3 What the evidence supports

The completed work supports the existence of a functioning local five-style system, saved repeated experiments, a larger provisionally labelled document bank and specific auditable repairs to extraction. It does not yet support a claim that the current bank has been completely inspected by independent people, that a modern candidate encoder has already improved the results, or that an arbitrary Kazakh document will be classified correctly. The earlier conference manuscript and the new experiments must be cited and described as separate stages; new runs cannot retroactively establish that a previously reported experiment was executed.

6 Remaining study and limitations

The next experiment is specified in configs/research_protocol_v7.json. The draft protocol requires a frozen corpus, declared label origins, reserved evaluation groups and identical registered input views. Its primary classifier set comprises three linear pipelines, mBERT, Kaz-RoBERTa last and mmBERT-small after a compatibility and memory probe. Layer concatenation, XLM-R and a local Kazakh LLM are optional extensions with separate hypotheses rather than an open-ended list of obligatory models.

A prospective reservation has now been registered before new training. It contains 1,000 candidate documents: 100 per style for document-group evaluation and 100 per style from source units withheld from the future v7 training and validation. Closing the embargo over complete canonical groups and reviewed exact-view passage links excludes {{EMBARGO_N}} documents. The initial exact-view preparation retains 997 candidates and quarantines three scientific excerpts for a language signal. The reference labels still require review; these candidates are not yet a final reference set.

{{RESERVATION_TABLE}}

The source units are Adilet, the KazNU ecology journal site, Abai, Adebiportal and the bookstore stratum of KazSAnDRA. This is a prospective source-withholding design: some websites were used in historical development. Related legal portals, KazNU journal sites and upstream review-platform strata must not be presented as five independent unseen publishers. A 400-document additional group-embargo budget per style preserves sufficient development material and limits representation of large author families. No scores from this reservation have been computed.

An AI-assisted diagnostic review has read 50 complete model views from the reservation, sampled across provisional style, source, genre and length. It provisionally retains 43 and requests further review of seven: three legal records dominated by title/repeal metadata, three excerpts with unresolved scientific/publicistic function, and one incomplete-looking opening already present in the saved original forum comment. No missing text is reconstructed. This targeted review does not estimate the corpus-wide label error rate. The 997 candidate views also produce zero flags against the 1,875 recorded v4/v6 inputs under the declared word-shingle overlap thresholds; paraphrases and encoder pretraining overlap are outside that check.

An additional audit against 25,259 development views flags three shared passages: two legal wording pairs and one scientific-method pair. Six complete displayed views were read before a conservative group embargo was extended. This removes six development records while preserving the original bank and every retained view unchanged. The resulting 25,253 development views produce zero flags against the 997 reserved views under the same thresholds. Shared boilerplate or a method description is not treated as proof that whole documents are duplicates.

mmBERT-small passes a discarded CUDA compatibility probe on the local 4 GB RTX 3050. At 256 tokens, microbatch one, FP16, gradient checkpointing and fused AdamW, all three optimizer updates succeed and peak allocated memory is 2.115 GiB. The input comes only from an eligible historical training row, explicitly projected to the three frozen tokenizer constraints. Probe weights are discarded and no validation or test predictions are obtained. This supports technical feasibility without providing an accuracy result or a guarantee for every subsequent batch.

The author has requested assistant-led corpus checking. Under that workflow, reference labels remain source-derived and AI-assisted unless independent human decisions are actually obtained. Reserved documents can be independent of this project's training split while their labels still lack human validation. A defensible dissertation must report both facts. A human audit of part of the reference set would strengthen external validity and may be requested by reviewers; software or an assistant cannot manufacture that evidence.

The main threats to validity are source and genre confounding, class-dependent lengths, incomplete work/author identification, uncertain provenance in imported informal datasets, possible overlap with encoder pretraining and limited generalisation from a fixed set of websites. The publication version also needs a completed literature review, the actual final evaluation, a consistent description of data rights, and confirmation of the existing conference paper's bibliographic status.

The final dissertation should contain the corpus construction chapter, the model and system chapter, completed experimental analysis, limitations and appendices with recipes, hashes and reproduction commands. University-specific front matter, formatting, defence dates and publication eligibility are administrative requirements to confirm for the current cohort. They are not inferred from an old publication plan or from qualification requirements applying to a scientific supervisor.

Conclusion of the working draft

KazStyle already provides the engineering and experimental foundation for the approved thesis topic. The immediate scientific priority is to finalise the data and evaluation conditions, not to promise a target F1 or repeatedly retrain an unchanged dataset. Completing those stages will allow the new English dissertation and an extended research article to rest on traceable results rather than unverified claims.
